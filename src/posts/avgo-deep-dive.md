Broadcom reported revenue of $29.6B for the quarter ended August 2, up 86% from a year ago. Its AI chip revenue nearly tripled. Management then told investors it expects AI revenue to roughly double next year and double again the year after. The stock fell anyway, and it's now about 28% below where it traded in June.

I don't own it yet. Here's how I'm thinking about it.

## What Broadcom actually sells

Most people think of Nvidia when they think of AI chips. Broadcom plays a different role. It co-designs custom accelerators (it calls them XPUs) with the companies that use them. Google's TPUs are the best-known example. It also makes most of the Ethernet switching chips that connect thousands of those accelerators inside a data center.

On top of that sits VMware, which Broadcom bought in 2023. VMware isn't exciting, but it grows steadily, earns high margins and pays for a lot of the dividend.

Here's how last quarter broke down:

| | Q3 FY26 revenue | vs. last year |
|---|---|---|
| Semiconductors | $20.8B | +127% |
| of which AI | $16.7B | +221% |
| Infrastructure software | $8.8B | +29% |
| Total | $29.6B | +86% |

There are six XPU customers. The ones management names are Google, Anthropic, OpenAI and Meta. On the call, they said Anthropic is likely to become the largest of them in 2027.

## Why I think the position is strong

Very few companies can design a leading-edge chip alongside a hyperscaler and then ship it in huge volumes. Broadcom has done it with Google for years, and that track record is the reason the newer AI labs came to it.

Networking matters more than people give it credit for. As clusters get bigger, the cost of connecting the chips grows faster than the cost of the chips themselves. Broadcom's AI networking revenue grew more than 2.5x in a year.

The part I find most interesting is supply. Lasers, HBM memory and substrates are all scarce right now. Management says it has already secured supply for its FY27 and FY28 targets. If that's true, it's a real advantage.

## The numbers

Last quarter Broadcom earned $3.32 per share on an adjusted basis, up from $1.69 a year ago. It turned 46% of revenue into free cash flow, $13.7B in a single quarter. For Q4 it guided to about $34.8B of revenue and $21.7B from AI.

The balance sheet carries about $59B of debt against $24B of cash, so roughly $35B of net debt. It's paying that down quickly: $7.1B during and just after the quarter. The dividend is $0.65 a quarter, a yield of about 0.75% at today's price.

## Is it cheap?

It depends which earnings number you use.

At around $346 a share, Broadcom is worth about $1.7T. Over the last four quarters it earned about $9.76 per share on an adjusted basis, which puts the stock at roughly 35 times earnings. That isn't cheap.

But management says it's on track for more than $30 of earnings per share in FY28. On that number, the stock is at about 11 or 12 times earnings, which would be very cheap for a business growing this fast.

So the question isn't whether Broadcom is a good business. It's how much of the FY28 plan actually happens on time. Here's how I'm thinking about the range. These are my own rough numbers, not a forecast:

| | What has to happen | FY28 EPS | Multiple | Price |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bad case | Deployments slip and AI revenue lands nearer $150B | ~$18 | 20x | ~$360 |
| Middle | Most of the plan happens, a bit late | ~$26 | 22x | ~$570 |
| Good case | Management hits its target | ~$32 | 25x | ~$800 |

Even the bad case isn't much below today's price. That's the main reason I'm interested.

## So why did it fall?

The Q3 numbers beat estimates. Q4 revenue guidance came in at $34.8B, slightly below the roughly $35.05B analysts wanted. For a stock priced on AI growth, a beat that small wasn't enough, and it's been weak since.

## What worries me

The biggest concern for me is financing. Broadcom is helping its customers pay for these chips. It set up a $35B vehicle with Apollo and Blackstone to fund Anthropic's 1 GW buildout, and management said it may provide residual value guarantees in some cases. When an analyst asked how large that exposure could get, management wouldn't say. When a supplier starts helping its customers buy its product, I want to understand exactly how much risk it's taking on.

There are a few other things:

- A handful of customers drive most of the growth, and two of the big AI labs depend on outside cloud and financing partners to pay their bills.
- Margins are slipping as the mix shifts toward memory-heavy chips and full rack systems. Gross margin went from about 77% to 75% and is guided to about 73%.
- Physical limits: land, power, buildings, lasers, memory. Management admitted that not all of the roughly 30 GW in the 2027–28 roadmap will reach production in that window.
- Customers could bring more of the design work in-house, or split orders with competitors.

## What I'm waiting for

I'd buy if three things happen: FY27 AI revenue tracks toward the $115B target, gross margins hold in the low 70s, and Broadcom says more about those financing guarantees.

I'd stay away if AI customers start pushing out deployments, or if Broadcom's balance sheet starts carrying real credit risk from its customers.

For now it's on the watchlist. The business is executing very well, but the risk has shifted from whether Broadcom can make the chips to whether its customers can pay for them and find the power to run them. I want more clarity on that before I put money in.
