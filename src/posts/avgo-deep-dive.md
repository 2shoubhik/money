## The thesis in three sentences

Broadcom designs custom AI accelerators (XPUs) and the Ethernet networking chips that tie AI clusters together, and it owns VMware, a cash machine. Management has guided AI semiconductor revenue to roughly double in each of the next two years, to **$115B in FY27 and $230B in FY28**, with supply already secured. The stock is down about 28% from its June high because the market doubts how much of that roadmap will actually land on time.

## The business

Broadcom reports two segments. For Q3 FY26 (quarter ended August 2, 2026):

| Segment | Q3 FY26 revenue | YoY growth |
|---|---|---|
| Semiconductor solutions | $20.8B | +127% |
| ↳ of which AI semiconductors | $16.7B | +221% |
| Infrastructure software (VMware etc.) | $8.8B | +29% |
| **Total** | **$29.6B** | **+86%** |

- **Custom XPUs.** Broadcom has six XPU customers. Named ones include Google (TPUs, under a multi-year supply deal), Anthropic, OpenAI and Meta. On the call, management said Anthropic should become the largest XPU customer in 2027.
- **AI networking.** Tomahawk switches and related chips. This revenue grew more than 2.5x year over year. Tomahawk 6 is ramping and Tomahawk 7 has taped out.
- **Software.** VMware and mainframe/enterprise software. It grows slowly, has high margins, and pays for the dividend.

## Why it wins

- **Custom silicon at scale is rare.** Few companies can co-design leading-edge accelerators with hyperscalers *and* ship them in volume. Customers get chips tuned to their models at a lower cost per unit of compute than buying merchant GPUs.
- **Ethernet is winning in AI networking.** Broadcom is the default supplier of Ethernet switching silicon. As clusters grow, networking takes a larger share of spend.
- **Secured supply.** Management says it has locked in supply (wafers, packaging, substrates) for the FY27–28 targets. That is a real advantage at a time when lasers, HBM and substrates are scarce.

## Valuation

At ~$346 per share and ~4.94B diluted shares, Broadcom has a **market cap of about $1.7T**.

- **Trailing non-GAAP EPS** is about $9.76 (Q4 FY25 $1.95 + Q1 $2.05 + Q2 $2.44 + Q3 $3.32). That puts the stock at **~35x trailing earnings**.
- Management says it's "very much on target" to exceed **$30 of EPS in FY28**. If that happens, today's price is **~11–12x FY28 earnings**.

The gap between those two multiples is the whole debate. The table below shows illustrative scenarios for FY28, about two years out:

| Scenario | What happens | FY28 EPS | Multiple | Implied price |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bear | Deployments slip, margins dilute further, AI revenue ~$150B | ~$18 | 20x | ~$360 |
| Base | Roadmap mostly lands, a little late | ~$26 | 22x | ~$570 |
| Bull | Delivers >$30 EPS as guided | ~$32 | 25x | ~$800 |

*These are my own rough assumptions to frame the risk/reward, not forecasts.*

## Why the stock fell

The Q3 report on September 2 beat estimates. But Q4 revenue guidance of $34.8B came in slightly below consensus (~$35.05B), and investors expected more from a company this exposed to AI. That came on top of the slide from the June high (~$480). The bar is very high: "great" isn't enough when the market already prices in "exceptional."

## Risks and what would change my mind

1. **Customer concentration.** A handful of AI labs and hyperscalers drive most of the growth, and two of the big labs depend on third-party cloud and financing partners to pay for the chips.
2. **Financing risk.** Broadcom is helping fund customer deployments, for example through a $35B financing vehicle with Apollo and Blackstone for Anthropic's 1 GW build. Management may provide residual value guarantees in some cases and declined to quantify that exposure. **This is the item I'd watch most closely.**
3. **Margin dilution.** Memory-heavy XPUs and full rack-scale systems carry lower gross margins. Gross margin is guided down from 75% to ~73%.
4. **Physical bottlenecks.** Land, power, data center shells, lasers, HBM. Management admits not all of the ~30 GW roadmap for 2027–28 will reach production in that window.
5. **Customers building in-house.** Hyperscalers could take more design work in-house or dual-source to rivals.

**What would make me buy:** evidence that FY27 AI revenue is tracking toward ~$115B, gross margins stabilising in the low 70s, and clearer disclosure on financing guarantees.

**What would keep me out:** AI customers pushing out deployments, or Broadcom's balance sheet taking on meaningful customer credit risk.

## My take

*(Rewrite this in your own voice.)* The business is executing extremely well, and at ~11–12x management's FY28 target, the upside is obvious *if* the roadmap lands. But the risk has shifted from "can they build it" to "can their customers pay for and power it." I'm watching rather than buying until the financing picture is clearer.
